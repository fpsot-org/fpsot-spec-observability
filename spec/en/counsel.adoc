= Questions for counsel
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

This page is informative.
It sets no rule, and it is not legal advice.
It lists the legal points that the research of {abbr-fpsot-first} raised and could not settle.
A lawyer must answer each one before {abbr-fpsot} or an operator relies on it.
Some article numbers below come from secondary sources, and nobody has checked them in the official text.
This page marks each of them as _to be verified_.

== Status of the Québec controls

1. Does the référentiel of security measures of the {abbr-cgcd-first} bind every public body, or only some kinds of bodies?
The question covers controls `AU-02`, `AU-03`, `AU-08`, `AU-11`, `AU-12` and `SC-45(01)`, which the sheets mark as mandatory.
The Québec act that governs information resources gives these controls their force.
The article of that act that does so is _to be verified_.
2. Does the Directive gouvernementale sur la sécurité de l’information set a format or a retention for logs?
Its article on the register of security events may do so, and its number is _to be verified_.

== Logs as personal information

1. Under the Québec access act, is an entry such as "employee X consulted file Y" personal information about both people?
Which article governs its collection, such as the rule on necessity?
The number of that article is _to be verified_.
2. Does a process-mining export with keyed pseudonyms remain de-identified information under the access act?
The number of the article on de-identified information is _to be verified_.
Could any realistic export meet the low risk that the Québec regulation on anonymization asks for?
3. After a destruction under the calendrier de conservation, are the salted leaf hashes of OBS-111 still personal information?
Are they public documents?
Can that design meet the Archives Act of Québec and the Québec act on information technology at the same time?
4. A study of workload or of the split of duties can use the performer of business events.
Is that a new purpose?
Would that purpose need its own privacy impact assessment and a consultation with the unions?
Where does such a study end, and where does the monitoring of staff begin?

== Requests from people

1. Must a Québec public body give a citizen the names of the employees who consulted their file?
The European Pankki S judgment answers no, except in rare cases, but it does not bind Québec.
The Commission d’accès à l’information decided a similar request at a hospital in 2005.
Nobody at {abbr-fpsot} has read its outcome.
2. The Québec access act limits access to documents that need a calculation or a comparison.
Does a query of logs by person count as such a calculation?
The numbers of the articles on this limit and on the scope of documents are _to be verified_.
Does a query that the software offers change the answer?
3. Federally, when do the Privacy Regulations treat an application audit log as used for an administrative purpose?
Paragraph `4(1)(a)` then keeps it for at least 2 years after its last use.
When is the log only security telemetry?

== Retention

1. Control `AU-11` sets 90 days online and 180 days in storage for audit records.
Are the 180 days added to the 90 days, for 270 days in all, or do they include them?
OBS-101 takes the added reading as the floor, which is how {abbr-fpsot} interprets the control.
Does the control also apply to records of business processes, which OBS-101 keeps for the same time?
2. How long must a public body keep the entries of its register of confidentiality incidents?
The research found 5 years, and that value is _to be verified_.
Must the logs that support an entry stay for the same time?

== The Cyber Resilience Act

1. A public body may deploy software of {abbr-fpsot} that records activity.
Who is then the user that may turn off that recording, under the European Cyber Resilience Act?
Point `(2)(l)` of Part I of Annex I sets that opt-out.
Is {abbr-fpsot} a manufacturer or an open-source software steward for these components?
