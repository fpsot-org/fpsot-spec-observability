= Privacy and minimization
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

These rules set what personal information a record may hold.
A record that says that an employee consulted the file of a citizen is personal information about both people.
In Québec and in federal law, such a record is also a document that the public body holds.

== What a record may hold

[[OBS-090]]
*OBS-090.* A component MUST write personal information only in the attributes that its inventory declares for it, under OBS-011.
The operator MUST turn off each such attribute that the {term-privacy-impact-assessment} of the system does not name.
The `Body` and the names of events, spans and metrics MUST NOT hold personal information.
Control `AU-03(03)` of the {abbr-cgcd-first} limits personal information in audit records to the elements that this assessment identifies.
That control is not mandatory, but mandatory control `AU-12` asks to remove personal information that an audit does not need.
A field that nobody declared is a field that nobody protects.

[[OBS-091]]
*OBS-091.* A record MUST NOT hold a password, a secret, a key, a token or a session cookie, under SEC-020.
It MUST NOT hold an authorization header, or the full content of a request or response with personal information.
A record MUST keep only the prefix of a client address, /24 for IPv4 and /48 for IPv6, in `org.fpsot.network.client.prefix`.
The FPSOT identity and access specification marks some events as needing the full client address.
Such an event MAY keep it in `client.address`, when the privacy impact assessment names it.
A full address points to a home or a device, while a prefix is enough to spot most attacks.

[[OBS-092]]
*OBS-092.* A record MUST name a person by an opaque internal identifier, under DAT-061 and DAT-062.
It MUST NOT name them by a name, an email address or an identifier that another body issues.
Examples of identifiers from another body are a social insurance number and a health insurance number.
A tool that shows records MAY look up the name for a reader who may see it.
The name then stays in one protected place, and the copies of the logs do not spread it.

== Process mining

[[OBS-093]]
*OBS-093.* An export for process mining MUST replace each identifier that leads to a person with a keyed pseudonym.
The pseudonym MUST be a link:https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc2104[keyed hash], such as HMAC-SHA-256, with a key for one purpose and one period.
It MUST keep at least 128 bits of the output, such as 32 hexadecimal digits.
A custodian outside the team of analysts MUST hold the key, and MUST destroy it when the period ends.
A tool MUST NOT use a hash without a key, since anyone can hash every possible file number and compare.
This pattern is the practice of {abbr-fpsot-first}, not the guidance of a regulator.

The performer of a step appears as a role, under OBS-075.
One key per export is the safest choice, since nobody can then join two exports.
The custodian of the key can be the privacy officer of the operator.

[[OBS-094]]
*OBS-094.* The documentation of a component and of a log store MUST state that pseudonymous records are still personal information.
It MUST NOT call an export anonymous, unless the operator has run the process that its law sets.
In Québec, a regulation on anonymizing personal information sets that process.
Federally, the link:https://www.canada.ca/en/treasury-board-secretariat/services/access-information-privacy/access-information-privacy-notices/2023-01-de-identification.html[notice of the Treasury Board on de-identification] says that de-identified information stays under the Privacy Act.
A false claim of anonymity leads people to share data that the law still protects.

== Requests from people

[[OBS-095]]
*OBS-095.* An access event MUST keep the purpose code of OBS-042 in its own attribute, apart from the actor.
A log store SHOULD let an authorized role find the audit events of one object, such as a person’s file.
The person can then learn when and why someone consulted the file, and the operator decides on employee names.
The European Court of Justice gave this right in the link:https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/upload/docs/application/pdf/2023-06/cp230107en.pdf[Pankki S judgment], which does not bind Canada.

== Tests

[[OBS-096]]
*OBS-096.* A test, a demonstration or a training environment MUST use synthetic data only, under TST-050.
Its records then hold no personal information from production.
A record of such an environment MUST NOT reach the log store of production.
The examples of this specification follow this rule, with addresses from link:https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc5737[RFC 5737] and made-up identifiers.
Logs of test systems often have weaker protection than production logs.
